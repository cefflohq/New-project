# CEFFLO BRAND VOICE & MALAYSIAN LANGUAGE SYSTEM — MASTER MD

**Status:** Founder Working Master — SOT Candidate
**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** Define how CEFFLO speaks across marketing content, product-led social content, narration, operator dialogue, educational content, captions, hooks and CTAs, with a Malaysia-first language system that sounds native rather than translated or AI-generated.

---

## 0. Founder Intent

CEFFLO must sound like a brand that understands real local delivery operations.

The voice must not sound like:
- generic SaaS marketing
- corporate advertising
- AI-written copy
- literal English-to-Malay translation
- government/formal Malay
- forced influencer slang

For Malaysia, CEFFLO should sound like natural Malaysian business conversation: clear, operational, human, calm and capable.

Depending on the scene, dialogue may use **bahasa pasar yang masih beradab**. Informality must come from the character and situation, not from forcing slang into every sentence.

Canonical Founder calibration sentence:

> **"Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"**

This sentence is a key reference for the natural rhythm expected in conversational Malaysian content.

---

## 1. Core Brand Voice

CEFFLO voice is:

### 1.1 Clear
Easy to understand immediately.

Avoid unnecessary explanation, jargon and long marketing sentences.

### 1.2 Operational
Speak from the reality of getting today's deliveries done.

Use concrete situations:
- orders
- drop points
- zones
- delivery plans
- runs
- riders/drivers
- dispatch
- customer updates
- issues
- delivered today

### 1.3 Calm
Operational chaos may exist in the scene, but CEFFLO itself does not panic.

CEFFLO brings structure.

### 1.4 Capable
Confident without boasting.

Do not rely on words such as "revolutionary", "game-changing" or "world-class" to sound competent.

Show capability through the situation and product.

### 1.5 Human
Sentences should sound speakable by a real person.

Natural rhythm is more important than textbook grammar in dialogue.

---

## 2. Master Voice Principle

> **CEFFLO sounds like an operator who understands the work — not a marketer trying to sell software.**

A viewer should feel:

> "Ya, memang macam ni kerja delivery kami."

before thinking:

> "Ini iklan software."

---

## 3. Malaysian Language Doctrine

### 3.1 Native Generation — Never Translation-First

Hard rule:

> **Never create the Malaysian Malay version by translating an English master copy.**

English and Malaysian Malay must be generated independently from the same:
- scenario
- intent
- Product Truth
- Content World
- operational pain
- CTA objective

Correct architecture:

```text
SCENARIO + INTENT + PRODUCT TRUTH
          ├── Native English output
          └── Native Malaysian Malay output
```

Incorrect architecture:

```text
English copy → literal translation → Malay
```

### 3.2 Translation Smell Test

Reject and rewrite from scratch if Malay:
- follows English sentence structure unnaturally
- uses technically correct words Malaysians rarely say in the situation
- sounds like subtitles translated automatically
- uses awkward metaphors imported from English
- feels grammatically polished but socially unnatural

Example — reject:

> "Bila delivery dah lebih 30 drop, cara anda menyusun kerja mula mengalami kerosakan."

Example — better:

> "Bila delivery dah banyak, nak susun satu-satu memang makan masa."

---

## 4. Malaysian Speech Register System

CEFFLO does not use one fixed formality level.

The system selects a register according to content type, speaker and situation.

### 4.1 Register A — Clean Natural

Use for:
- product demo
- official announcement
- product walkthrough
- formal B2B-facing content
- explanatory UI/product narration

Example:

> "Semua delivery hari ni dah disusun ikut zone."

> "Delivery plan dah siap. Tinggal assign rider."

Professional, but not bureaucratic.

### 4.2 Register B — Everyday Business

**Default social-content register.**

Use for:
- Reels
- TikTok
- UGC-style content
- owner/operator scenes
- most problem-solution videos
- practical educational content

Example:

> "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"

> "Rider dah keluar, customer mula tanya order sampai mana."

> "Hari ni delivery banyak sikit. Ada 70 lebih."

Natural Malaysian business speech.

### 4.3 Register C — Pasar Beradab

Use selectively for:
- skits
- POV
- staff interaction
- owner/rider dialogue
- high-realism operational scenes

Allowed when natural:
- je
- ni
- dah
- nak
- kejap
- eh
- ha
- okay
- lah
- tu
- kan

Examples:

> "Kejap, rider ni dah penuh. Tinggal lagi 12 order."

> "Eh, yang ni siapa hantar? Customer dah tanya ni."

> "Yang area Shah Alam ni letak sekali lah. Takkan nak pergi dua kali."

> "Yang ni... kejap. Area mana?"

The objective is believable speech, not slang performance.

---

## 5. Respect Boundary

Natural does not mean rude.

Default prohibited or strongly discouraged in official CEFFLO voice:
- aku
- kau
- korang
- bro
- wei/weh
- beb
- gila as filler
- bodoh
- aggressive mockery
- insulting customer/operator language

These may not be inserted merely to create a Malaysian identity.

Direct-address pronouns should often be omitted entirely when the sentence is more natural without them.

Instead of:

> "Anda mempunyai 50 order dan anda perlu mengagihkan order tersebut kepada rider anda."

Prefer:

> "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"

Use **anda** only where direct respectful address genuinely improves clarity.

---

## 6. Slang Rule

> **Slang follows the character and situation. Slang is never added just to manufacture Malaysian identity.**

A factory dispatcher, bakery owner, rider and formal product narrator do not need identical speech.

Character dialogue may be more colloquial.

Brand narration remains cleaner.

Do not make every sentence contain `lah`, `je`, `ni` or hesitation markers.

---

## 7. Spoken Naturalness

Dialogue may contain:
- hesitation
- interruption
- incomplete sentences
- short responses
- self-correction
- pauses
- repetition where natural
- reaction words

Examples:

> "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"

> "Yang Bangi ni banyak."

> "Ha, tu lah."

> "Kejap... yang ni customer nak sebelum pukul dua."

These features may improve realism for UGC/skits.

They must not be mechanically injected into every script.

---

## 8. Speaker Voice Matrix

### 8.1 CEFFLO Brand / Narrator

Tone:
- clear
- calm
- concise
- capable
- slightly cleaner than character dialogue

Example:

> "Susun ikut zone. Siapkan delivery plan. Assign rider."

Avoid excessive slang.

### 8.2 Owner / Operator

Tone:
- natural
- practical
- thinking about today's workload
- may use Everyday Business or Pasar Beradab

Example:

> "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"

### 8.3 Staff / Dispatcher

Tone:
- short
- situational
- action-oriented
- naturally colloquial

Example:

> "Yang Bangi ni banyak."

> "Rider ni dah penuh."

> "Okay, yang ni settle."

### 8.4 Rider / Driver

Tone:
- brief
- operational
- respectful
- natural

Example:

> "Yang ni last drop area Kajang kan?"

Do not caricature riders.

### 8.5 Educational Voice

Tone:
- knowledgeable
- accessible
- useful before sales
- conversational but structured

Example:

> "Bila delivery dah banyak, masa paling banyak biasanya habis dekat kerja susun dan bahagi order."

### 8.6 Product Demo Voice

Tone:
- minimal
- factual
- product-first

Example:

> "48 order. Susun ikut zone, siapkan run, kemudian assign rider."

---

## 9. Operational Vocabulary

Use the words Malaysians in the target business context would naturally use.

Common acceptable vocabulary:
- order
- delivery
- rider
- driver
- route
- drop
- drop point
- zone
- run
- assign
- dispatch
- customer
- WhatsApp
- status
- area
- pickup
- delivered
- issue
- database
- plan
- dashboard

Do not translate an established everyday operational term merely to make the sentence "more Malay".

Context determines whether Malay or English terminology sounds more natural.

---

## 10. Anti-Corporate / Anti-AI Vocabulary

Avoid by default:
- revolutionary
- game-changing
- cutting-edge
- seamless
- empower
- unlock
- transform your business
- next-generation
- innovative ecosystem
- elevate
- supercharge
- robust solution
- comprehensive solution
- AI-powered, unless AI itself is truthful and materially relevant
- optimize/optimumkan when a simpler concrete phrase works

Do not use abstract adjectives where a concrete operational outcome can be shown.

Instead of:

> "Transform your delivery operations seamlessly."

Prefer:

> "Susun order, bahagi ikut rider, terus dispatch."

---

## 11. Rhythm & Sentence Construction

Prefer:
- short clauses
- spoken rhythm
- concrete nouns
- operational verbs
- specific numbers where truthful
- natural connectors such as `tapi`, `lepas tu`, `kalau`, `bila`
- occasional pauses where appropriate

Avoid:
- long subordinate clauses
- over-explaining
- formal essay structure
- slogan in every sentence
- excessive punctuation
- forced one-word fragments everywhere

Numbers are useful because they make situations tangible.

Example:

> "Ada 68 order nak keluar sebelum tengah hari. Rider ada empat."

---

## 12. Hook Doctrine

Hooks should sound like a real problem or thought, not an advertising headline.

### Natural Problem Hook

> "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"

### Time Pressure

> "Pukul 10 dah. Lagi 40 delivery belum keluar."

### Customer Pressure

> "Rider tengah jalan, customer dah mula tanya order sampai mana."

### Capacity

> "Rider ni dah penuh. Tinggal lagi 12 order."

### Manual Work

> "Setiap pagi masih copy alamat satu-satu?"

### Operational Question

> "Yang area Bangi ni siapa nak ambil?"

Do not force clickbait.

---

## 13. Dialogue Doctrine

Dialogue exists to create believable operational moments.

It must not become an obvious sales script.

Bad:

> Owner: "I am struggling with my delivery operations."
>
> Staff: "Why don't you use CEFFLO?"
>
> Owner: "Great idea!"

Reject.

Better:

> Owner: "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"
>
> Staff: "Yang Bangi ni banyak."
>
> Owner: "Ha, tu lah."

Cut to real CEFFLO product.

Brand VO:

> "Susun ikut zone. Siapkan run. Assign rider."

Then return to operation.

This lets the product solve the moment rather than having characters advertise it unnaturally.

---

## 14. Product Introduction Rule

Do not make characters unnaturally explain CEFFLO features to each other.

Prefer:

`problem scene → real product proof → operational outcome`

rather than:

`character says product marketing copy`

The UI should carry part of the explanation.

---

## 15. CTA System

CTAs should be low-pressure and operational.

Examples:

> "Urus delivery hari ni dengan Cefflo."

> "Susun delivery. Assign rider. Jalan."

> "Bila order makin banyak, biar operation tetap tersusun."

> "See how Cefflo handles today's deliveries."

Avoid:
- BUY NOW
- ACT NOW
- TRANSFORM YOUR BUSINESS TODAY
- DON'T MISS OUT
- excessive urgency without a real reason

CTA language should match the content register.

---

## 16. Content World Adaptation

The same brand voice adapts to the world.

### Meal Prep

> "Ada 80 meal nak sampai sebelum tengah hari. Rider ada empat je."

### Online Seller

> "Order masuk banyak hari ni. Sekarang nak kena susun siapa hantar area mana."

### Bakery / Gifts

> "Petang ni ada 36 delivery. Yang ni pula customer nak sebelum pukul tiga."

### Catering

> "Tiga event, lima lokasi, semua timing lain-lain."

### Distributor

> "Empat van. Banyak drop point. Nak bahagi ikut area dulu."

### Factory / Warehouse

Use slightly cleaner operational language where appropriate:

> "Hari ni ada 67 drop point. Empat driver available. Kita susun ikut area dulu."

Do not make every industry sound like a TikTok teenager.

---

## 17. English Voice

English follows the same brand principles:
- operational
- direct
- calm
- human
- non-corporate
- native English, not translated Malay

Example:

> "50 orders. Three riders. How are you splitting today's deliveries?"

Not:

> "Leverage CEFFLO to seamlessly optimize your last-mile delivery ecosystem."

English and Malay can differ in sentence construction while preserving the same intent.

---

## 18. Multilingual Expansion Rule

For future languages:
- do not translate word-for-word
- create native-language voice calibration
- preserve operational intent
- use locally natural business vocabulary
- establish respect/formality rules for that language
- use native review before production scale

The Malaysian Malay rules must not be blindly copied to Mandarin, Tamil or another market.

---

## 19. Subtitle & On-Screen Copy

Subtitle language should preserve spoken naturalness.

Do not "correct" natural dialogue into formal Malay if doing so changes the character voice.

Keep subtitles:
- readable
- concise
- synchronized with speech
- free from unnecessary filler when readability requires trimming

On-screen product labels must reflect actual CEFFLO UI.

Do not invent UI labels for the sake of copy consistency.

---

## 20. Voiceover Direction

AI or human voiceover should avoid:
- announcer voice
- radio-ad energy
- exaggerated enthusiasm
- robotic pacing
- overly perfect corporate pronunciation

Desired:
- conversational
- confident
- natural pace
- appropriate Malaysian rhythm for BM
- subtle pauses
- context-aware emotion

Character dialogue and brand narration may use different voices.

---

## 21. Language QA — Hard Gate

Every Malaysian Malay script must pass:

### Q1 — Would a Malaysian naturally say this?
If no → rewrite.

### Q2 — Does it smell translated from English?
If yes → reject and regenerate natively.

### Q3 — Is it unnecessarily formal?
If yes → simplify.

### Q4 — Is it trying too hard to sound local?
If yes → remove forced slang.

### Q5 — Is it respectful?
If no → rewrite.

### Q6 — Does it sound like marketing copy rather than an operational situation?
If yes → rewrite.

### Q7 — Can a real actor/creator say it comfortably aloud?
If no → rewrite.

### Q8 — Does terminology match how the target business actually speaks?
If uncertain → research/review rather than guess.

---

## 22. Malaysian Naturalness Scoring

Before creative approval, score 1–10:

- native Malaysian feel
- spoken naturalness
- respectfulness
- operational authenticity
- clarity
- character appropriateness
- absence of translation smell
- absence of generic AI/corporate language

Any critical failure in translation smell, respectfulness or Product Truth is an automatic reject regardless of average score.

## 25. AI Prompting Requirements

Every content-generation request that produces Malaysian speech should include structured context:

- speaker role
- business world
- operational situation
- pain
- audience
- selected register: A/B/C
- product truth
- purpose of line
- desired emotional state
- words/claims to avoid
- relevant approved calibration examples

Do not simply prompt:

> "Make this sound Malaysian."

That encourages stereotypes and forced slang.

---

## 26. DeepSeek Language Role

The primary Content Intelligence model should generate each language natively from structured intent.

For BM:
1. understand scenario
2. select appropriate speech register
3. write from Malaysian operational context
4. run Translation Smell self-check
5. run respect check
6. run corporate/AI-language check
7. output structured script
8. send to Language/Creative QA

Do not expose chain-of-thought. Store only useful QA result, score, flags and corrected output.

---

## 27. Learning System

Language performance should also enter Marketing Memory.

Track where practical:
- language
- register
- speaker type
- hook wording
- slang density/class
- dialogue vs narration
- performance metrics
- QA score
- Founder approval/rejection
- Founder correction
- correction reason

Founder corrections are high-value calibration evidence.

If Founder repeatedly changes a phrase pattern, update the language calibration rules rather than treating each correction as isolated.

## 30. Acceptance Tests

### Test A — Meal Prep / Everyday Business
Input:
- 50 orders
- 3 riders
- today's delivery allocation

Expected naturalness should be comparable to:

> "Ada 50 order tapi rider ada tiga je. Hmm... macam mana nak bahagi delivery hari ni?"

Reject if output resembles formal translation.

### Test B — Staff Dialogue / Pasar Beradab
Input:
- rider capacity full
- 12 orders remain

Expected direction:

> "Kejap, rider ni dah penuh. Tinggal lagi 12 order."

### Test C — Product Demo / Clean Natural
Input:
- delivery plan complete
- rider assignment pending

Expected direction:

> "Delivery plan dah siap. Tinggal assign rider."

### Test D — Factory
Input:
- 67 drop points
- 4 drivers
- route grouping

Output must sound operational and credible for a B2B environment, not juvenile or influencer-like.

### Test E — Translation Trap
Give the system an English corporate sentence and request Malaysian content.

Expected:
- preserve intent only
- rewrite from scenario
- do not translate sentence structure

### Test F — Forced Slang Trap
Request "make it very Malaysian."

Expected:
- system uses configured register
- does not inject `bro`, `weh`, `korang`, excessive `lah`

### Test G — Respect
No canonical CEFFLO narration should address the audience using `aku/kau`.

## 32. Definition of Done

The CEFFLO Brand Voice & Malaysian Language System is operational when the engine can receive a structured scenario, select the appropriate speaker/register, produce native Malaysian dialogue or narration, reject translation-smelling/corporate/forced-slang output, preserve Product Truth, and deliver language that sounds believable when spoken aloud.

Success is not:

> "The Malay is grammatically correct."

Success is:

> **"This sounds like something a real Malaysian business operator would actually say."**

# Canonical Final Rule

> **Natural, not translated. Casual when the situation allows it, but always beradab. Operational before promotional. Human before polished.**
