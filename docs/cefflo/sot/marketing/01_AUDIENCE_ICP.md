# CEFFLO — AUDIENCE & ICP
**Status:** Canonical Marketing Knowledge SOT
**Version:** 1.0 — 2026-09-04
**Authority:** Founder → Brand Brain → Product Truth → this document

## 1. Purpose
Define who Cefflo should attract, which operating situations make a business a strong fit, what those people care about, and how marketing should recognize qualified attention.

This document defines **hypotheses and targeting doctrine**, not fabricated market research. Marketing Memory must validate/refute these hypotheses with real evidence.

## 2. Core ICP Definition
The strongest-fit Cefflo business is not defined by industry. It is defined by an operating pattern:

> A business receives multiple local orders, manages delivery inside its own service area, uses its own/vendor-controlled riders or delivery operation, and needs a clearer way to move today's orders from intake through planning, dispatch, execution and completion.

### Strong-fit signals
- recurring local same-day delivery;
- multiple orders per operating day;
- multiple destinations/stops;
- owner/team currently coordinates delivery manually or semi-manually;
- delivery work must be grouped, assigned, sequenced or monitored;
- business owns the customer relationship;
- business controls or coordinates its riders;
- delivery volume creates operational mental load;
- customer-status questions or delivery exceptions consume attention;
- the owner wants operational control rather than a marketplace.

### Weak-fit / non-ICP signals
- no meaningful local delivery operation;
- only occasional one-off courier booking;
- wants Cefflo to supply an open rider marketplace/network;
- wants a marketplace to acquire customers;
- primarily needs accounting/payment/CRM software;
- needs nationwide parcel logistics rather than own-service-area operations;
- expects Cefflo to manage vendor-customer payments.

## 3. Primary Decision-Maker
### Vendor / Owner / Operator
Usually the person who feels the delivery chaos directly and can decide to adopt a new operating system.

Jobs:
- know what came in today;
- know what is ready;
- know where orders belong;
- plan delivery work;
- coordinate riders;
- see what is moving;
- resolve exceptions;
- know what has been delivered.

Emotional drivers:
- control;
- fewer things to remember;
- confidence that nothing is missed;
- less back-and-forth;
- clearer next action;
- ability to handle more volume without multiplying chaos.

## 4. Secondary Influencers
### Operations / Helper
Cares about preparation, packing, readiness and handoff clarity.

### Rider
Cares about clear assigned work, stops, pickup, run progression and fewer ambiguous instructions.

### Customer
Not the SaaS buyer, but their tracking/status experience affects vendor trust and perceived professionalism.

Marketing may show these perspectives to demonstrate the connected operation, while primary acquisition remains Vendor/Owner-led unless evidence later supports another buying motion.

## 5. Initial Segment Families
These are scenario families, not permanent vertical silos.

### A. Food / Meal Operations
Examples: meal prep, bakery, catering-like local drops, home/commercial food businesses.
Likely situations: batches, readiness timing, many same-day addresses, peak periods.

### B. Florist / Gift / Hamper
Likely situations: date-sensitive deliveries, multiple stops, customer-status sensitivity, vehicle/handling considerations.

### C. Beauty / Skincare / Local Retail
Likely situations: local order batches, social-commerce order sources, owner-managed riders, repeat delivery areas.

### D. Other Local Delivery Operators
Any business matching the operating model should remain eligible.

Rule: never let one segment become the master positioning.

## 6. Operating-Maturity Segments
### ICP-1 — Manual Starter
Uses WhatsApp, paper, memory or ad hoc lists.
Pain: owner is the system.

### ICP-2 — Spreadsheet Operator
Uses Excel/Sheets to organize orders but dispatch/execution remains fragmented.
Pain: spreadsheet records information but does not run the delivery operation.

### ICP-3 — Growing Local Fleet
Has several riders/team members and recurring multi-drop work.
Pain: coordination complexity grows faster than headcount.

### ICP-4 — Structured Operator
Already has processes/tools but has a gap between orders, preparation, delivery planning, rider execution and customer visibility.
Pain: disconnected systems/hand-offs.

Do not claim which segment converts best until Marketing Memory proves it.

## 7. Trigger Events
High-intent moments may include:
- order volume suddenly increases;
- owner adds riders;
- business expands service area;
- festive/peak campaign creates delivery spikes;
- repeated missed/late/confused deliveries;
- owner is spending too much time assigning riders;
- customer status messages become excessive;
- business moves from occasional to daily delivery;
- spreadsheet workflow starts breaking;
- owner wants to professionalize operations without joining a marketplace.

## 8. Core Pain Library
P01 — too many orders to mentally track
P02 — orders from scattered sources
P03 — address/location friction
P04 — uncertainty about service-area coverage
P05 — orders need geographic/operational grouping
P06 — assigning riders one by one
P07 — rider/vehicle/capacity mismatch where relevant
P08 — unclear stop sequence
P09 — preparation and dispatch out of sync
P10 — peak-hour owner bottleneck
P11 — rider repeatedly asks for instructions
P12 — customer repeatedly asks for status
P13 — multi-drop run complexity
P14 — delivery exception/recovery consumes attention
P15 — owner cannot see the whole operation today
P16 — volume rises while process remains manual
P17 — fear of changing an existing spreadsheet/WhatsApp workflow

Each pain used in marketing must be framed as a plausible operating situation, not an invented customer quote.

## 9. Objection Library
O01 "WhatsApp dah cukup."
O02 "Aku cuma ada 1–2 rider."
O03 "Delivery aku local je."
O04 "Aku dah guna Excel/Google Sheets."
O05 "Tak nak ubah cara team kerja."
O06 "Tak nak app yang complicated."
O07 "Customer aku order dari banyak tempat."
O08 "Aku bukan food business."
O09 "Aku boleh assign rider sendiri."
O10 "Kenapa perlukan system kalau rider tahu kawasan?"

Response doctrine: acknowledge the existing workflow, reveal the operational cost/gap, then show relevant product truth. Never insult manual methods or imply every small business needs Cefflo.

## 10. Awareness Stages
### Unaware
Content: recognizable operational situations.

### Problem-aware
Content: explain why delivery chaos occurs and why volume changes the problem.

### Solution-aware
Content: teach the operating-system model: coverage, zones, plan, runs, riders.

### Cefflo-aware
Content: real product demonstrations and workflow evidence.

### Trust / Evaluation
Content: objections, real proof, limitations, setup clarity, relevant comparisons.

### Intent / Conversion
Content: current CTA only—early access, try, register, subscribe, enquire, etc., according to actual launch state.

## 11. Jobs-to-be-Done
"When today's local orders increase, help me turn them into clear delivery work so my team and riders know what happens next without me carrying the whole operation in my head."

Supporting jobs:
- organize today's work;
- make delivery boundaries visible;
- turn orders into manageable runs;
- coordinate preparation and dispatch;
- give riders clear execution context;
- surface exceptions;
- give customers truthful visibility.

## 12. Message-to-Audience Matrix
Owner overwhelmed → operational clarity / Today view
Spreadsheet operator → records are not execution; show workflow connection
Growing rider team → clear runs/assignments, not rider marketplace
Peak-volume operator → structured planning/review
Customer-status pain → truthful tracking where supported
Non-food operator → demonstrate category-neutral operating model

All capability-specific examples remain subject to Claims Registry.

## 13. Qualification Signals for Analytics
High-quality engagement may include:
- profile/site visits from business operators;
- comments describing similar delivery pain;
- questions about setup, riders, zones, planning or pricing;
- early-access/signup/demo intent;
- repeat visits;
- saves/shares by likely operators;
- direct enquiries with real operating context.

Views alone are not qualification.

## 14. Research Questions for Marketing Memory
The engine should learn:
- which operating-maturity segment converts best;
- which pain creates strongest qualified response;
- which industries are over/underrepresented;
- whether owners respond more to control, time, scale or customer experience;
- which objections block conversion;
- what volume/complexity threshold predicts need;
- which platform reaches qualified operators;
- which words customers naturally use for the problem.

## 15. Anti-Assumption Rules
Never invent:
- market size;
- persona demographics;
- income/revenue;
- fleet size distribution;
- conversion rate;
- "most businesses" claims;
- preferred platform;
- buying cycle.

Store these as hypotheses until real evidence exists.

## 16. Definition of Done
An agent using this SOT can determine:
- whether a business is likely ICP;
- which pain/trigger/objection applies;
- which awareness stage to address;
- what message direction fits;
- what remains a hypothesis requiring Marketing Memory evidence.
