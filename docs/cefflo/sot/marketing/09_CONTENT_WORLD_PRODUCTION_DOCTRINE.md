# CEFFLO CONTENT WORLD & CONTENT PRODUCTION DOCTRINE — MASTER MD

**Status:** Founder Working Master — Pre-Implementation SOT Candidate
**Purpose:** Define the content-world, production, realism, scenario-generation, learning-loop, and model-evaluation doctrine that must exist before CEFFLO AI Content Engine production implementation.
**Implementation rule:** Audit and reconcile against newer repository truth before adoption. Do not silently supersede newer Founder decisions.

---

## 0. Founder Intent

CEFFLO content must not look like generic AI advertising.

The audience should recognize real operational situations: businesses receiving many orders, managing customer/order data, grouping many delivery drop points, planning routes/runs, assigning riders/drivers, dispatching, tracking deliveries, handling exceptions, updating customers, and completing today's local deliveries.

AI is production infrastructure, not the visual aesthetic.

The product shown in content must be the real CEFFLO product. Generated footage may create believable environments, people, vehicles, packaging, and operational moments, but must never fabricate CEFFLO UI, features, proof, customer results, integrations, or operational capabilities.

---

## 1. Canonical Positioning Boundary

CEFFLO is a **local same-day delivery operating system for businesses that run their own local delivery operation**.

The category of the business is not the boundary. The operating model is.

Canonical operational arc:

`many local orders → coverage → zones → delivery plan → multi-drop runs → riders/drivers → dispatch → tracking/customer communication → delivered today`

CEFFLO is not positioned as a rider marketplace or an independent rider network. Content must not imply that CEFFLO supplies riders unless a future Founder-approved product decision explicitly changes that boundary.

---

## 2. Content Engine Objective

The engine must produce content that:

1. Makes a target operator immediately recognize a real delivery-operations problem.
2. Shows or explains how CEFFLO fits that operating reality.
3. Uses real CEFFLO product evidence where product functionality is shown.
4. Feels native to TikTok/Reels/social feeds rather than like stock SaaS advertising.
5. Can be produced repeatedly at high volume without becoming visually random.
6. Learns which business worlds, pains, hooks, formats, and creative treatments perform.
7. Feeds those learnings back into future production.
8. Preserves Founder approval and Product Truth/Brand QA boundaries.

The engine is a learning system, not a random content generator.

---

## 3. CEFFLO Content World System

A **Content World** is a persistent, believable business environment representing a class of operators with recurring people, places, assets, workflows, constraints, and delivery problems.

### 3.1 Launch Core Worlds

Start with six broad worlds. They are deliberately wider than individual micro-verticals.

#### CW-01 — Meal Prep / Prepared Food
Typical reality:
- tens to hundreds of prepared meals
- morning/lunch delivery windows
- recurring customers
- dense local drops
- rider capacity and timing pressure
- temperature/time sensitivity

#### CW-02 — Online Seller / Social Commerce
Typical reality:
- orders from online channels, chat, forms, or internal systems
- customer/order data spread across tools
- local same-day fulfillment
- address handling
- batching and rider assignment
- customer asking for delivery status

#### CW-03 — Bakery / Gifts / Florist / Hampers
Typical reality:
- fragile or presentation-sensitive items
- seasonal spikes
- birthday/event/time-window deliveries
- many same-day addresses
- peak-day rider pressure
- failed/late delivery has high customer impact

#### CW-04 — Catering / Events
Typical reality:
- multiple delivery destinations
- strict arrival windows
- large or mixed loads
- vans/cars/motorbikes
- schedule changes
- coordination between kitchen/operations/drivers

#### CW-05 — Retail / Supplier / Distributor
Typical reality:
- store-to-customer or supplier-to-outlet deliveries
- recurring routes
- multiple branches/customers
- vehicle capacity
- driver allocation
- proof/status visibility

Includes examples such as fresh produce, frozen food, beverage, pet supplies, parts, water and other local distribution operations.

#### CW-06 — Factory / Warehouse / Local B2B Distribution
Typical reality:
- larger order database
- dozens of drop points
- vans/drivers
- branch/outlet/customer delivery
- manifests and delivery planning
- route efficiency
- operational visibility
- end-of-day completion

### 3.2 Expansion Worlds

Do not launch all of these simultaneously. Add them based on market evidence:

- laundry pickup/drop
- pharmacy/health retail where claims/compliance allow
- beauty/skincare local fulfillment
- corporate gifting/documents
- spare-parts distribution
- grocery/fresh produce
- frozen/chilled delivery
- beverage restocking
- specialty retail
- other businesses whose core pattern is many local orders → many drop points → own delivery resources

### 3.3 Persistent World Bible

Each active Content World should eventually have a reusable world bible containing:

- business name/persona identity
- business type
- approximate scale
- location archetype
- owner/operator reference
- staff references
- rider/driver references
- vehicles
- packaging
- uniforms where applicable
- interior/exterior environment
- phone/device style
- recurring operational constraints
- typical order volume
- typical delivery windows
- recurring visual motifs
- approved reference assets
- prohibited inconsistencies

The purpose is visual continuity. Do not generate unrelated random owners, stores, riders, packaging, or vehicles for every episode.

## 4. Operational Pain Taxonomy

Content should be generated from operational pains, not merely business categories.

### OP-01 — Order Intake Chaos
Examples:
- WhatsApp orders
- spreadsheet imports
- manual entry
- orders from multiple channels
- missing/dirty address data

### OP-02 — Order Database / Operational Visibility
Examples:
- unclear list of today's orders
- duplicate/manual records
- difficult status tracking
- no single operational view

### OP-03 — Too Many Drop Points
Examples:
- 20/40/80/100+ local destinations
- operator cannot mentally organize the day
- drops need grouping

### OP-04 — Coverage / Service Area
Examples:
- deciding what can be served today
- addresses outside service area
- operational boundary confusion

### OP-05 — Zoning / Batching
Examples:
- grouping nearby drops
- balancing operational areas
- preparing work before route/run creation

### OP-06 — Route / Delivery Plan
Examples:
- manual Google Maps planning
- copying addresses one by one
- inefficient sequencing
- too much time spent planning

### OP-07 — Rider/Driver Assignment
Examples:
- which rider takes which run
- rider availability
- vehicle suitability
- workload distribution

### OP-08 — Capacity / Multi-Drop Runs
Examples:
- rider already full
- too many drops for one run
- multiple riders/vehicles
- splitting delivery work

### OP-09 — Dispatch & Handover
Examples:
- rider waiting for instructions

### OP-10 — Live Delivery Visibility

### OP-11 — Customer Updates

### OP-12 — Delivery Exceptions

## 5. Scenario Generation Engine

DeepSeek V4 Pro or the approved primary intelligence model should not receive prompts such as:

> "Create a florist video."

The engine should compose scenarios from structured variables.

### 5.1 Core Formula

`Business World × Operational Pain × Scale × Constraint × Persona × Hook Type × Content Format × Product Proof × CTA`

Example:

`Meal Prep × Route Planning × 86 drops × must arrive before lunch × owner/operator × pain hook × Problem→Product→Outcome × real Delivery Plan UI × soft CTA`

Another:

`Factory/Distributor × Rider Assignment × 42 drops × 4 vans × dispatcher × numerical hook × Product Demo × real Runs UI × demo CTA`

### 5.2 Scale Variables

Examples:
- 18 orders
- 32 orders
- 57 orders
- 86 orders
- 120 orders

Use believable scale for the selected world. Do not inflate numbers merely for drama.

### 5.3 Constraint Variables

Examples:
- before 12 PM
- same afternoon
- three riders only
- one rider unavailable
- multiple zones
- customer changed address
- seasonal rush
- fragile items
- recurring B2B drops
- van capacity
- delivery window

### 5.4 Hook Families

Examples:
- numerical pain
- operator confession
- "still doing this manually?"
- before/after
- POV
- operational question
- mistake
- time-pressure
- customer complaint
- rider problem
- spreadsheet/WhatsApp pain

The system must maintain a hook library and performance history rather than inventing from zero every time.

## 6. V1 Video Focus

Do not attempt to focus equally on every content type at launch.

### 6.1 Primary — Real-World Problem → Product → Outcome

**Target share: ~45%**

Structure:
1. recognizable operational pain
2. believable real-world scene
3. real CEFFLO product intervention
4. operational outcome
5. concise CTA

This is the primary acquisition format.

### 6.2 Secondary — Product-Led Workflow / Proof

**Target share: ~35%**

Use actual CEFFLO UI captures.

Examples:
- today's orders
- zones
- delivery plan
- multi-drop runs
- rider assignment
- dispatch
- ongoing/delivered states

Purpose: prove that CEFFLO is a real operating product rather than an abstract claim.

### 6.3 Supporting — Operator Education

**Target share: ~20%**

Examples:
- when to divide deliveries into zones
- how to think about 30+ same-day drops
- why manual route planning breaks at scale
- rider allocation mistakes
- how customer-status questions create operational overhead

Educational content should teach something useful even if the viewer does not immediately buy.

### 6.4 Non-Priority at Launch

Use sparingly:
- generic cinematic brand films
- random trends/memes
- motivational founder content
- long narrative films
- generic AI spectacle
- unrelated viral formats

They may be tested later through controlled experiments.

## 8. Real Product Truth Rule

Any shot that claims to show CEFFLO functionality must use approved real product assets whenever possible.

Approved sources may include:
- real screen recordings
- approved screenshots
- exported UI assets
- controlled motion treatment built from real product captures

Video/image models must not fabricate:
- CEFFLO screens
- buttons
- maps
- routes
- analytics
- customer messages
- integrations
- product results
- features

Generated device/environment footage may provide framing around a real composited product capture, provided the final output remains truthful.

If the engine cannot source approved product evidence, it must choose a non-product visual treatment rather than hallucinate the interface.

---

## 9. Shot-Level Production Doctrine

Do not normally ask one generative video model to create an entire finished 20–30 second CEFFLO advertisement.

A finished video should be assembled from controlled assets.

Example 10-shot composition:

**Generated/filmed-style assets**
1. owner checking phone
2. staff packing
3. rider collecting orders
4. rider leaving

**Real/controlled assets**
5. CEFFLO Orders
6. CEFFLO Zones
7. Delivery Plan
8. Runs/Dispatch
9. typography/result
10. CEFFLO CTA/end card

The ratio is not fixed. Product-led videos may use almost entirely real UI. Scenario videos may use more generated environment footage.

---

## 10. Standard Short-Form Structure

Default target duration: **15–30 seconds**, unless experiment data supports another length.

Typical structure:

- **0–2s:** Hook
- **2–7s:** Problem/context
- **7–18s:** Product/operational solution
- **18–25s:** Outcome/proof
- **25–30s:** CTA

Do not enforce this rigidly when a better native-social edit exists.

Primary aspect ratio for short-form video: **9:16**.

## 14. AI Video Model Selection Gate

**Do not lock a production video provider based solely on leaderboard position, marketing demos, or advertised price.**

Candidates may include current viable models such as MiniMax, Seedance, Veo, Runway/Kling or others, but the actual shortlist must be verified against current official API availability and pricing at evaluation time.

### 14.1 CEFFLO Benchmark

Run the same CEFFLO production scenarios through shortlisted models.

Minimum benchmark set should include:

1. Meal-prep packing + owner phone interaction
2. Rider pickup / loading / departure
3. Online-seller order-pressure scene
4. Warehouse/distributor multi-drop preparation
5. Natural human dialogue/UGC shot where applicable
6. Reference-consistency test using a persistent character/environment

### 14.2 Scoring Dimensions

Score each 1–10:

- human realism
- natural motion
- hand/object integrity
- environmental realism
- "does not look AI" score
- prompt adherence
- reference-image adherence
- character consistency
- location consistency
- mundane-action quality
- vertical 9:16 usability
- editing/control capability
- generation speed
- failure/retry rate
- API reliability
- automation suitability
- cost per usable shot
- commercial production usability

### 14.3 Most Important Metric

Do not optimize for **cost per generation**.

Optimize for:

> **cost per usable approved shot**

A cheap model that requires six retries may be more expensive than a higher-priced model that produces an approved shot immediately.

### 14.4 Decision Outcome

After benchmark:

- select **Primary Video Model**
- optionally select **Economy/Fallback Video Model**
- document exact reasons
- document failure cases
- retain provider abstraction in n8n

Do not create five production dependencies when one primary and one fallback are sufficient.

`01_AUDIENCE_ICP.md` §5's own rule — "never let one segment become the master positioning" — applies equally to Content Worlds: no single world may become the master visual identity of CEFFLO.

## A4. Scenario Formula ↔ Angle Formula Relationship (Founder decision 2)

`03_CONTENT_PHILOSOPHY.md` §7: `Pillar × Situation × Angle × Audience × Hook × Format × Product Truth × Platform` remains the cross-platform (including text-native Threads) content-philosophy formula.

This document §5.1: `Business World × Operational Pain × Scale × Constraint × Persona × Hook Type × Content Format × Product Proof × CTA` is its **video/Content-World-specific specialization** — Business World is a situated instance of Pillar×Situation; Scale/Constraint are video-production-specific refinements with no counterpart in the Angle formula. Both formulas coexist: the Angle formula governs Master Concept Builder's output for all platforms; the Scenario formula operates specifically where a Master Concept is realized as Content-World-based video (inside the Meta/TikTok creative lanes).
