# PART A — GLOBAL COUNTRY & LANGUAGE ARCHITECTURE

## A1. Core Decision

**Country/market and language are two separate settings. They must not be permanently coupled.**

Cefflo is Malaysia-first commercially, but the product architecture must be capable of expanding internationally without creating separate country-specific apps.

Target model:

```text
ONE CEFFLO PRODUCT FAMILY
        ↓
Market / Country Context
        +
Independent User Language
        ↓
Localized Vendor / Driver Experience
```

Do not create separate Malaysia, Indonesia, Singapore, etc. app variants merely to support localization.

---

## A2. First-Launch Language Behaviour

On first launch:

```text
Device / OS language
        ↓
Is that language supported by Cefflo?
        ├─ YES → use supported device language
        └─ NO  → fallback to English
```

Country must **not** force the interface language.

Example:

```text
Country: Indonesia
Language: English
```

is valid.

Likewise:

```text
Country: Malaysia
Language: Bahasa Melayu
```

and:

```text
Country: Malaysia
Language: English
```

are both valid.

After first launch, the user must be able to change language manually from Settings.

The explicit user choice must be persisted and should take priority over future automatic device-language detection unless the user resets the preference.

---

## A3. Country / Market Responsibilities

Country/market context may govern market-specific behaviour such as:

- phone country code / phone formatting;
- currency;
- timezone defaults;
- address/locale formatting;
- market availability;
- regional pricing configuration;
- market-specific legal/commercial configuration where required;
- other country-level operational configuration explicitly supported by Cefflo.

Country must **not** be treated as the permanent source of UI language.

Do not assume that every person in a country wants the same interface language.

---

## A4. Language Responsibilities

Language controls user-facing interface content, including where applicable:

- navigation;
- labels;
- buttons;
- forms;
- validation messages;
- empty states;
- errors;
- onboarding/authentication copy;
- settings;
- operational statuses;
- help/support copy;
- notification copy generated by the app;
- other translatable product strings.

Language selection must not alter canonical operational data or business logic.

---

## A5. User-Level Language Preference

Language preference is **per user**, not necessarily per business.

Example:

```text
Business: ABC Bakery Malaysia

Owner / Vendor user → English
Operations staff     → Bahasa Melayu
Driver A             → Bahasa Melayu
Driver B             → English
```

All may participate in the same Cefflo business while seeing their own supported interface language.

Do not make a business-wide language setting override every user unless a separate business-content language feature is intentionally introduced later.

---

## A6. Launch Language Scope

For the initial Malaysia launch, prioritize complete, production-quality localization for:

1. **Bahasa Melayu**
2. **English**

Do not ship partially translated core flows merely to advertise a large language count.

Additional languages should be introduced progressively based on expansion needs and translation QA.

Architecture should be extensible so additional locale files can be added without restructuring the product.

---

## A7. Product Surfaces

Apply the localization architecture to relevant authenticated product surfaces, especially:

- Vendor Flutter Mobile;
- Driver Flutter Mobile;
- Vendor Web/Desktop where the current architecture supports it.

Customer Tracking and Public Website should use their own appropriate localization strategy and must not be forced into an authenticated-user preference model if unnecessary.

Founder Admin remains an internal surface and should not block customer-facing localization work.

---

## A8. Settings UX

Country and Language must appear as conceptually separate settings.

Example:

```text
REGION
Country / Market     Malaysia

PREFERENCES
Language             Bahasa Melayu
```

Do not present one combined control such as:

```text
Malaysia — Bahasa Melayu
```

as the only possible selection.

The language selector should clearly display language names in a user-recognizable form.

---

## A9. Localization Engineering Requirements

Claude must inspect existing localization/i18n code before implementation.

Target characteristics:

- no hard-coded duplicated translation system if Flutter/web localization already exists;
- stable locale keys;
- English fallback;
- persistent explicit language choice;
- no business logic keyed from translated display strings;
- formatting separated from translation where appropriate;
- tests for fallback and persistence;
- no missing-string crashes;
- no silent mixing of languages in core launch flows.

Where supported by the current stack, use platform-standard locale identifiers.

---

## A10. Localization Acceptance Criteria

At minimum verify:

1. Fresh install with supported device language selects that language.
2. Unsupported device language falls back to English.
3. User can manually switch BM ↔ English.
4. Manual language selection survives app restart/login lifecycle as appropriate.
5. Country can remain Malaysia while language is English.
6. Country can remain Malaysia while language is BM.
7. Changing language does not mutate business country, currency, operational data, zones, orders, riders, or delivery state.
8. Two users belonging to the same business can use different languages.
9. No untranslated/hard-coded strings remain in the core launch flows covered by the implementation.
10. Existing authentication and operational tests remain green.

# PART B — AI CREATOR / UGC MARKETING STANDARD

## B1. Strategic Decision

Cefflo Marketing may operate **Cefflo-owned AI creator personas** as scalable content creators.

These personas are not to be represented as fabricated real customers.

Model:

```text
Cefflo Marketing AI
        ↓
AI Creator Personas
        ↓
TikTok / Instagram / Facebook / Threads
        ↓
Attention
        ↓
Traffic / Lead
        ↓
Vendor Signup
        ↓
Activated Vendor
        ↓
Paid Vendor
```

The objective is not “generate lots of posts.” Marketing output must ultimately connect to qualified attention, signup, activation and paid conversion.

---

## B2. Why AI Creator Personas

Founder direction:

- avoid depending on paid human UGC talent for routine scalable content;
- human creator cost and AI production/render cost should not both become mandatory for every creative;
- build repeatable AI-native creator identities that Cefflo can operate continuously;
- maintain quality high enough that content feels natural rather than like a generic AI advertisement.

AI creators can function similarly to recurring content creators working for the Cefflo marketing operation.

---

## B3. Persona Consistency

Each approved creator persona should maintain a stable identity across content:

- face;
- approximate age range;
- voice;
- accent/language behaviour;
- personality;
- speaking style;
- visual identity;
- recurring business/operational angle.

Do not randomly regenerate a visibly different “same creator” every post.

Potential persona lanes may include, for example:

- F&B / restaurant operator angle;
- bakery / meal-prep operator angle;
- delivery operations / business tips angle.

The final persona count and exact identities should be controlled by the Marketing content system, not improvised independently in every generation.

---

## B4. Malaysian Voice Standard

For Malaysia-first content:

- natural Malaysian Bahasa Melayu;
- natural English mixing only where it fits the persona;
- conversational delivery;
- avoid TV-commercial announcer tone;
- avoid overly formal textbook BM;
- allow natural pauses and rhythm;
- use believable local phrasing without turning every script into slang caricature.

Scripts should sound like a person talking to a phone camera, not a corporate voice-over reading a brochure.

---

## B5. Human Behaviour Standard

AI creators should display natural:

- eye movement;
- blinking;
- facial micro-expression;
- subtle smiles/reactions;
- head movement;
- hand/body movement;
- pauses;
- speech tempo variation;
- intonation.

Avoid:

- frozen avatar posture;
- perfect lip-sync look with unnatural face motion;
- repeated robotic gestures;
- permanently fixed eye contact;
- synthetic “presenter” behaviour unless the content format specifically calls for it.

---

# PART C — CONTROLLED IMPERFECTION / SMARTPHONE UGC VISUAL STANDARD

## C1. Principle

**UGC must not look like a cinematic corporate commercial.**

Target impression:

> A believable person casually recorded this on a normal modern smartphone.

The desired quality is **controlled imperfection**, not poor production quality.

---

## C2. Capture Look

Default UGC direction:

- vertical 9:16;
- smartphone / iPhone-like capture;
- normal consumer-camera rendering;
- slight handheld micro-movement;
- realistic framing;
- moderate detail;
- natural exposure;
- natural white balance;
- ordinary dynamic range;
- natural motion blur during movement.

Avoid making every UGC video look:

- ultra-sharp;
- heavily HDR;
- excessively contrasty;
- over-saturated;
- perfectly stabilized;
- studio-lit;
- cinema-camera graded;
- glossy corporate advertising.

---

## C3. Skin & Face

Require:

- believable skin texture;
- visible natural facial detail;
- no plastic/waxy skin;
- no excessive beauty filter;
- no hyper-perfect facial symmetry caused by generation;
- consistent identity across shots where the same creator appears.

---

## C4. Camera Imperfections Allowed

Small realistic imperfections are desirable when controlled:

- subtle autofocus adjustment;
- tiny exposure adaptation;
- imperfect headroom;
- slightly off-centre framing;
- minor handheld movement;
- natural motion blur;
- realistic phone-camera response to mixed lighting.

Do not deliberately add severe degradation, fake compression damage, extreme shake or distracting artifacts.

---

## C5. Lighting

Prefer believable everyday environments:

- window light;
- shop lighting;
- kitchen lighting;
- office lighting;
- café/restaurant environment;
- car interior when contextually appropriate;
- ordinary indoor/outdoor daylight.

Not every creator video should look like it was filmed in a professional studio.

# PART D — CONTENT STRATEGY

## D1. Launch Funnel

Use a layered creative funnel:

```text
1. Problem-first UGC
        ↓
2. Problem → Solution UGC
        ↓
3. Product Proof / Demo
        ↓
4. Corporate / Brand Trust
        ↓
5. Real Customer UGC / Testimonials
   once genuine customers and evidence exist
        ↓
6. Conversion / Offer Ads
```

The corporate film is a reusable trust asset, not the only daily acquisition format.

---

## D2. Problem-First Content

Cefflo should first make the target business recognize its operational problem.

Relevant themes:

- orders increase but delivery coordination becomes messy;
- owner loses track of which rider has which orders;
- too many delivery points to manage manually;
- zones/riders/orders become difficult to coordinate;
- growth creates operational chaos.

Do not make every post start with “Use Cefflo.”

---

## D3. Cefflo Product Truth in Content

When Cefflo is demonstrated, reflect the real operating model:

```text
Orders
  ↓
Coverage
  ↓
Zones
  ↓
Delivery Plan
  ↓
Multi-drop Runs
  ↓
Riders / Drivers
  ↓
Delivered Today
```

Do not turn Cefflo into a generic courier, food marketplace, rider marketplace or on-demand delivery aggregator in marketing storytelling.

---

## D4. Content Variety

Approved creator content can include:

- POV;
- short story;
- operational chaos scenario;
- owner/business pain;
- educational tip;
- humorous observation;
- problem/solution;
- product demonstration;
- reaction-style content;
- workflow explanation;
- before/after operational scenario, provided no fabricated performance result is claimed.

Cefflo does not need to be hard-sold in every video.

# PART E — TRUTH, CLAIMS & DISCLOSURE

## E1. No Fake Customer Testimony

AI creator persona ≠ real customer.

Before real customer evidence exists, do not script claims such as:

- “Aku dah guna Cefflo 6 bulan” if untrue;
- fabricated revenue improvements;
- fabricated delivery-cost savings;
- fabricated customer logos;
- fabricated order-volume outcomes;
- fabricated testimonials;
- fabricated performance statistics.

An AI creator may discuss a realistic scenario, demonstrate the product, explain a problem, or present Cefflo as a solution without pretending to have a real customer history.

---

## E2. Real Customer Phase

Once real vendors exist, genuine customer stories can be added only when:

- customer identity/use is genuine;
- permission is obtained where required;
- quoted results are evidence-backed;
- metrics are not exaggerated;
- claims comply with the Cefflo claims registry and current marketing rules.

---

## E3. Platform AI/Synthetic-Media Rules

Before automated publishing of photorealistic AI-human content, Marketing must verify the **current official disclosure/synthetic-media rules** for each destination platform.

Do not hard-code assumptions about TikTok, Meta, Instagram, Facebook or other platform disclosure requirements into this document because policies can change.

Publishing workflow must be capable of adding required labels/disclosures where applicable.

# PART F — MARKETING AUTOMATION & GOVERNANCE

## F1. Automation Direction

Target workflow:

```text
Concept
  ↓
Script
  ↓
Storyboard / Prompt
  ↓
AI Creator Render
  ↓
Brand + Claims + Human-Naturalness QA
  ↓
Caption / Voice / Music / Assembly
  ↓
Risk / Approval Gate
  ↓
Publish
  ↓
Metrics
  ↓
Learning Loop
```

Rendering requires an actual connected video-generation provider/service. Do not mark the render stage operational merely because prompts/scripts can be generated.

---

## F2. Risk Levels

Routine content generation under locked brand/content rules may operate autonomously.

Scheduled publishing may operate within predefined limits with audit evidence.

Escalate for Founder approval where the existing risk policy requires it, including consequential budget or positioning changes.

Do not give Marketing broad production/database/deployment permissions merely for convenience.

---

## F3. Metrics

Do not use “number of posts generated” as the final success metric.

Track the chain:

```text
Content
  ↓
Distribution
  ↓
Qualified Attention
  ↓
Lead
  ↓
Vendor Signup
  ↓
Activated Vendor
  ↓
Paid Vendor
```

Creative-level metrics can still be used for optimization, but business output remains the end goal.
