# 1. MASTER POSITIONING

## 1.1 Canonical positioning

> **Cefflo is a local same-day delivery operating system for businesses
> that manage deliveries within their own service area.**

Cefflo helps businesses coordinate the operational work required to move
many local orders from order intake to preparation, planning, rider
execution and customer delivery.

The boundary is the **operating model**, not the product category.

Cefflo may serve businesses such as:

-   food businesses;
-   bakeries;
-   meal-prep businesses;
-   florists;
-   gifts and hampers;
-   beauty and skincare businesses;
-   local retailers;
-   other businesses operating their own local same-day delivery
    workflow.

These examples are not category restrictions.

## 1.2 Core operating model

**MANY LOCAL ORDERS\
→ COVERAGE\
→ DELIVERY ZONES\
→ DELIVERY PLAN\
→ MULTI-DROP RUNS\
→ RIDERS\
→ DELIVERED TODAY**

A broader end-to-end experience can be understood as:

**ORDER SOURCES\
→ ORDERS\
→ PREPARE\
→ PACK\
→ READY\
→ PLAN\
→ MULTI-DROP RUNS\
→ RIDERS\
→ CUSTOMER\
→ DELIVERED TODAY**

## 1.3 Plain-language explanation

> **Cefflo helps businesses plan, run and track the local deliveries
> they manage themselves.**

## 1.4 Product narrative

> **Sell anywhere. Bring orders into Cefflo. Prepare them with your
> team. Plan today's deliveries. Give riders clear runs. Keep customers
> informed. See the whole operation from one place.**

## 1.5 Grow doctrine

**Grow = Operate.**

At launch, Cefflo grows by helping businesses operate local same-day
delivery better.

Growth is not positioned as abstract business growth software. The
product earns the right to help businesses grow by making their daily
operation more controlled, visible and repeatable.

# 2. WHAT CEFFLO IS

Cefflo is:

-   a local same-day delivery operating system;
-   an operational coordination layer;
-   a system for businesses that manage deliveries inside their own
    service area;
-   a connected workspace for owner/vendor, operations/helper, rider and
    customer;
-   a system for moving today's orders from intake to delivered;
-   a way to create operational clarity across people, orders and
    delivery work;
-   vendor-centric: the business owns its customer relationship, brand
    and delivery operation.

Cefflo's job is to make the operation clearer:

> **One operation. Everyone knows what happens next.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. WHAT CEFFLO IS NOT

Cefflo is **not**:

-   a marketplace;
-   a rider marketplace;
-   a courier company;
-   a delivery fleet supplied by Cefflo;
-   GrabFood;
-   Foodpanda;
-   Lalamove;
-   a generic CRM;
-   a generic order-management SaaS;
-   a payment processor between vendor and customer;
-   an accounting/invoicing platform;
-   a home-food-only operating system;
-   a food-category-first product.

Cefflo does not need to own the rider or the customer channel.

> **Your brand stays in front. Cefflo works behind it.**

> **Keep selling the way you already do.**

# 5. PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE --- FOUR CONNECTED WORKSPACES

Cefflo is one operation experienced through four connected perspectives.

## 5.1 Vendor / Owner --- CONTROL

Purpose:

> **Control the operation.**

The Vendor/Owner needs to know:

-   what came in today;
-   what is being prepared;
-   what is ready;
-   what needs planning;
-   who is delivering;
-   what is moving;
-   what is delayed or needs attention;
-   what has been delivered.

Core areas include:

-   dashboard / Today at a Glance;
-   orders;
-   order detail;
-   manual order creation;
-   current deliveries;
-   riders/team;
-   delivery sessions and assignments;
-   delivery stops;
-   zones;
-   coverage;
-   planning / review and dispatch;
-   operational attention/recovery;
-   storefront;
-   products/menu;
-   appearance;
-   business/profile/settings;
-   notifications where supported.

Vendor is not merely an admin panel. It is the operational command
center.

## 5.2 Operations / Helper --- PREPARE

Purpose:

> **Prepare the work.**

Core operational loop:

**ORDER → PREPARE → PACK → READY**

Operations/Helper should focus on fulfillment work rather than
whole-business control.

Core areas include:

-   preparation workspace;
-   packing;
-   preparation status;
-   live preparation/session visibility;
-   readiness;
-   checklists/tasks where supported;
-   helper/team workflows.

Operations/Helper remains a first-class product role. It must not be
silently removed simply because a screen or marketing section focuses on
Vendor/Rider/Customer.

## 5.3 Rider --- EXECUTE

Purpose:

> **Execute the delivery.**

Canonical Rider experience:

**PLAN ROUTE → PICKUP CHECKLIST → DELIVERY RUN**

Core areas include:

-   assigned run;
-   run details;
-   stops;
-   current stop;
-   pickup;
-   navigation;
-   delivery progression;
-   arrival;
-   next stop;
-   completion;
-   proof of delivery where supported;
-   exceptions/recovery where supported.

Rider is an execution interface, not a business-management dashboard.

## 5.4 Customer --- ORDER + TRACK

The Customer experience has two main phases.

### Before delivery --- Storefront / Ordering

-   vendor-branded customer ordering surface;
-   product/menu browsing;
-   ordering where supported;
-   vendor identity remains primary.

### During delivery --- Customer Tracking

-   order identity;
-   delivery status;
-   delivery progression;
-   ETA only when truthfully supported;
-   rider/contact context where supported;
-   map/location context where supported;
-   order details;
-   completion;
-   rating where supported.

Customer Tracking is not an accounting or payment-management portal.

# 6. CAPABILITY TRUTH SYSTEM

Every meaningful product capability must be classified before it is
represented publicly or treated as implemented.

## 6.1 Status definitions

### LIVE

Implemented and sufficiently real to be represented as an existing
capability.

A backend object or dormant RPC alone does not automatically make a
complete user-facing workflow LIVE.

### LOCKED / IN DEVELOPMENT

Founder-approved direction or committed product capability that is being
built, reconciled or completed, but must not yet be marketed as fully
live.

### FUTURE

A roadmap capability intentionally deferred beyond the current product
stage.

### IDEA / EXPLORATION

A concept under consideration with no commitment to ship.

### OUT OF SCOPE

Explicitly outside the current Cefflo product responsibility and should
not be reintroduced without Founder approval.

## 6.2 Current high-level capability map

### Vendor / Owner

**LIVE / established foundation** - vendor authentication; - operational
dashboard foundations; - orders list/detail; - real manual order
creation path; - recent orders; - order status/progression; - current
delivery data; - customer tracking token generation; - canonical rider
data; - delivery-session/assignment/stop data; - zone backend
foundations; - storefront; - products/menu; - four curated storefront
themes: Clean, Warm, Fresh, Midnight; - storefront preview/share where
supported.

**LOCKED / IN DEVELOPMENT** - complete zone UI wiring; - real
invitation/workforce reconciliation; - coverage area; - address/location
capture; - out-of-coverage indication; - delivery planning / Review &
Dispatch; - deterministic planning; - rider capacity; - Need
Attention; - Live Operations surfaces; - recovery / post-pickup
reassignment; - persistent vendor branding gaps where applicable.

**FUTURE** - CSV / Excel order import; - provider-based WhatsApp/SMS
delivery notifications.

**IDEA / EXPLORATION** - API/POS integrations unless later
verified/approved; - automatic route optimization; - AI route
planning; - arbitrary storefront brand-color control beyond approved
theme system.

### Operations / Helper

**LIVE / established foundation** - preparation workflow; - packing
workflow; - live preparation/session foundations; - prepare → pack →
ready progression.

**LOCKED / IN DEVELOPMENT** - stronger helper/team model; -
approval/workforce reconciliation; - structured vendor-owned helper
workflows where defined.

**FUTURE** - public helper discovery; - cross-vendor helper
marketplace; - nationwide helper matching.

### Rider

**LIVE / established foundation** - rider PWA/auth foundations; -
assigned delivery/run data; - stop data; - current-stop surface; - run
details; - pickup/delivery workflow foundations; - proof-of-delivery
foundations; - customer rating foundations; - multi-stop backend/data
foundations.

**LOCKED / IN DEVELOPMENT** - reliable complete multistop UI wiring; -
Plan Route experience; - Pickup Checklist; - Delivery Run UX; -
rider-controlled stop sequence where approved; - critical slide
interactions; - real GPS/location writes; - live rider location/map; -
exception handling; - recovery.

**IDEA / EXPLORATION** - automatic route optimization; - AI route
optimization.

### Customer

**LIVE / established foundation** - vendor storefront; - product/menu
browsing; - customer ordering foundations; - curated vendor theme
presentation; - customer tracking; - tracking token; - delivery
status/progression; - POD/rating foundations where exposed.

**LOCKED / IN DEVELOPMENT** - honest ETA; - richer live-location
tracking dependent on real Rider GPS.

**FUTURE** - automated delivery notifications; - WhatsApp/SMS
notification provider integration.

**OUT OF SCOPE** - invoice; - quotation; - outstanding-balance
management; - vendor-customer payment management; - generic accounting
features.

## 6.3 Claim rule

Marketing, product copy and agent output must never silently convert:

-   LOCKED / IN DEVELOPMENT → LIVE;
-   FUTURE → LIVE;
-   IDEA → committed roadmap;
-   backend foundation → complete user-facing feature.

When uncertain, verify repository truth or label the uncertainty.

# 8. VISUAL IDENTITY

## 8.1 Core palette

Neutral structural foundation:

-   **Warm White:** `#F5F5F0`
-   **Pure White:** `#FFFFFF`
-   **Soft Graphite:** `#E9E9E3`
-   **Cefflo Black:** `#0D0E0D`
-   **Graphite:** `#171917`
-   **Signal Lime:** `#C7F000` --- current signature candidate/direction
-   **Hero Near-White:** `#F7F7F2`
-   **Dark Secondary Text:** approximately `#A4A69F`
-   **Light Border:** approximately `#DADBD4`
-   **Dark Border:** approximately `#303236`

Exact secondary neutrals may be normalized through the implementation
token system, but must preserve this visual direction.

## 8.2 Signal Lime semantics

Signal Lime means:

-   active;
-   live;
-   current;
-   ready;
-   selected;
-   moving;
-   primary operational action.

Signal Lime is not a decorative filler color.

Do not blindly replace every legacy purple element with lime.

Map color according to meaning.

## 8.3 Lime contrast rule

Do not use Signal Lime as ordinary body text on white/warm-white
backgrounds where contrast is inadequate.

Prefer lime for:

-   filled operational controls with appropriate foreground;
-   indicators;
-   selected navigation states;
-   active signals;
-   knobs/handles;
-   concise status/action accents.

## 8.4 Superseded visual direction

Purple and blue are no longer Cefflo's primary/signature brand
direction.

Legacy purple/blue styling should be reconciled rather than preserved
merely because it exists.

# 9. UI SYSTEM

UI is part of the brand.

Cefflo's UI should feel like the interface of a real local operation:
fast to scan, quiet, structured and confident.

## 9.1 UI character

The canonical character is:

-   clean;
-   operational;
-   structured;
-   quiet;
-   precise;
-   mobile-native;
-   high-confidence;
-   fast to scan.

Avoid:

-   gaming aesthetics;
-   crypto/neon aesthetics;
-   glassmorphism;
-   purple-tech SaaS styling;
-   decorative gradients without operational purpose;
-   excessive card nesting;
-   oversized empty layouts;
-   lime glow;
-   arbitrary visual novelty.

## 9.2 Light and Dark

Cefflo supports two modes:

-   Light;
-   Dark.

**Light is the structural master.**

Dark mode should preserve the same:

-   content order;
-   dimensions;
-   hierarchy;
-   alignment;
-   vertical rhythm;
-   gutters;
-   card padding;
-   row heights;
-   gaps;
-   typography sizing/weight;
-   icon sizing;
-   button/input/chip/navigation geometry.

Dark mode changes theme properties, not the information architecture.

## 9.3 Spacing

Use a coherent 4px-derived rhythm.

Directional scale:

-   4;
-   8;
-   12;
-   16;
-   20;
-   24;
-   32;
-   40;
-   48. 

Typical relationships:

-   4--8: micro spacing;
-   8--12: tightly related label/value;
-   12--16: internal row/component spacing;
-   16--20: component-to-component;
-   24--32: major section separation.

Primary mobile gutter direction: approximately 16px, increasing
carefully on wider screens.

Avoid random one-off spacing.

## 9.4 Typography hierarchy

Directional hierarchy:

-   page title: \~24--28;
-   major operational value / ETA: \~28--32;
-   section title: \~14--16;
-   card primary: \~16--18;
-   row primary: \~14--16;
-   body/supporting: \~13--14;
-   metadata: \~11--13.

Implementation should normalize against existing application typography
rather than blindly hardcoding these numbers.

## 9.5 Cards and lists

Cards are for grouped operational context.

Do **not** place every list item inside its own floating card.

For dense operational lists, prefer:

-   clear row hierarchy;
-   compact metadata;
-   divider-based separation;
-   restrained status;
-   clear affordance.

### Vendor Recent Orders baseline

Use the approved compact pattern:

-   clear order ID;
-   small supporting item count;
-   restrained status pill;
-   right-side chevron/action affordance;
-   minimal separators;
-   clean View All treatment.

Rows should read as an operational list, not a stack of decorative
cards.

## 9.6 Borders, radii and shadows

-   subtle neutral borders in Light;
-   restrained graphite borders in Dark;
-   normalized radius families;
-   subtle shadows;
-   Dark mode should prefer background/border elevation;
-   no glowing lime borders;
-   no purple glow.

## 9.7 Iconography

Icons should be:

-   simple;
-   legible;
-   operational;
-   consistent in visual weight;
-   not ornamental.

Rider navigation uses a sharp, solid navigation/dispatch pointer
treatment with Signal Lime when representing the approved active
navigation action.

# 10. UX SYSTEM

UX should reduce uncertainty and make the next operational action
obvious.

## 10.1 Core UX principle

At every stage, the user should be able to answer:

-   What is happening?
-   What needs my attention?
-   What should I do next?
-   What changed after I acted?

## 10.2 Operational hierarchy

Prioritize:

1.  current state;
2.  next action;
3.  exception/attention;
4.  supporting detail;
5.  historical/secondary information.

Do not make users decode decorative UI to understand operational state.

## 10.3 Rider safety interactions

Critical Rider actions use a **slide mechanism**, not a simple tap,
where the approved workflow calls for it.

Critical actions include:

-   Start Pickup;
-   Start Delivery;
-   Arrive;
-   Next Stop;
-   Complete Order.

Purpose:

-   reduce accidental activation;
-   reduce ghost touches;
-   improve confidence in wet/rain/outdoor conditions;
-   make high-consequence transitions intentional.

Approved slider direction:

-   long dark track/card;
-   white action label;
-   truly circular Signal Lime knob;
-   clear directional arrow/affordance.

In Light Mode, the critical slider may remain dark because it is a
safety/action component rather than a generic theme surface.

## 10.4 Feedback states

Every meaningful interaction should account for:

-   loading;
-   success;
-   empty;
-   error;
-   disabled;
-   confirmation where consequential;
-   retry/recovery where appropriate.

Never fabricate success.

A success toast must correspond to a real completed action.

## 10.5 Responsive behavior

Cefflo applications are real responsive PWA/web products, not hardcoded
iPhone mockups.

Validate at practical mobile widths such as:

-   360px;
-   390--430px;
-   430--480px;

and tablet/desktop where the surface supports it.

Check:

-   safe areas;
-   bottom navigation;
-   sticky actions;
-   slider;
-   keyboard interaction;
-   forms;
-   sheets/modals;
-   scrolling;
-   overflow;
-   maps.

## 10.6 Accessibility

Require:

-   adequate contrast;
-   careful lime usage;
-   visible focus states;
-   semantic controls;
-   meaningful labels;
-   reasonable touch targets;
-   readable Dark mode;
-   clear disabled states;
-   reduced-motion handling.

Accessibility is a product-quality requirement, not a post-launch
decoration.
